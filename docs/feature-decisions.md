# Feature decisions

These 35 product decisions record the intended scope reached through iterative design review. They preserve the reasoning behind implementation choices; they do not claim every feature is built. Current status is in the README.

## Confirmed decisions

| Area | Decision |
|---|---|
| Deployment hosts | A single Linux VM with an isolated Docker Compose project; portable, explicitly operated releases. Host inventory and actual deployment history stay private |
| Review method | One feature at a time, with keep/remove/alternative choices |
| Feature 1: meal text processing | Keep hybrid: local parsing for recognized formats/confirmed aliases/templates; external AI only for unfamiliar or complex wording. Local/manual logging remains available during AI outages. |
| Feature 2: portion accuracy and estimate approval | Suggest portions from personal history or typical averages, but save only with a sufficiently precise user-provided/confirmed quantity or an explicit Telegram button tap approving the displayed rough estimate. |
| Feature 3: meal-photo logging | Keep photo-assisted meal drafts in v1. Identify foods and suggest portions, then obtain precise amounts or explicit estimate approval before saving. |
| Feature 4: corrections | Keep full editing, deletion, and undo through conversational replies and Telegram buttons. Support correcting quantities, food selections, and dates; recalculate affected totals. |
| Feature 5: favorites and recipes | Keep both named favorite/repeat meals and ingredient-based batch recipes. Store ingredients and cooked yield/serving definitions; calculate logged portions deterministically. |
| Feature 6: packaged foods | Keep both barcode lookup (typed digits or clear barcode photo) and nutrition-label photos with reviewed extraction/manual correction. Resolve the product and serving basis separately from the amount consumed. |
| Feature 7: calories and macros | Keep calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat for saved meals and daily totals. Guided targets and adjustment behavior are recorded under features 12 and 13. |
| Feature 8: fiber and micronutrients | Keep fiber and all available micronutrients, including sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, iron, zinc, vitamins D/B12 and other supported nutrients. Show source-data coverage; missing values remain unknown, not zero. |
| Feature 9: daily progress | Keep the full on-demand daily view via `/today` or natural-language questions: compact calories/macros versus configured targets, remaining amounts, nutrient overview, and expandable meal/nutrient detail. Reminders and target-setting rules remain separate decisions. |
| Feature 10: meal suggestions | Keep full on-demand suggestions from saved meals/recipes and other verified foods: 2–3 practical options with portions based on remaining targets, supported nutrient gaps, and preferences. Suggestions are not consumed entries until explicitly logged. |
| Feature 11: body-weight tracking | Keep quick weight logging, measurement history, seven-day average, and multiweek rate of change when enough measurements exist. Calorie adjustments follow feature 13. |
| Feature 12: goals and initial targets | Keep guided selection of fat loss, maintenance, or lean gain and desired pace, with suggested initial calorie/macro targets for review and explicit application. Retain manual overrides and the ability to supply known targets. |
| Feature 13: adaptive calorie targets | Keep weekly evidence-gated reviews and small explained adjustment proposals. Apply changes only after explicit user acceptance; insufficient logs or single-day weight changes do not justify adjustments. Notification timing remains to be selected. |
| Feature 14: weekly report | Keep the full weekly nutrition/weight report and an on-demand short version. Full view includes daily intake, complete-day averages, adherence, nutrient coverage, logging completeness, weight trend, and any adjustment proposal; short view highlights key results and actions. |
| Feature 15: quick gym logging | Keep a simple path such as “chest and triceps for 40 mins.” Save reported session focus/duration without requiring exercise details; use clearly labeled typical estimates for missing effort/load information, preferring comparable user-reported history when available. |
| Feature 16: detailed gym logging | Keep optional exercises, sets, reps, lifted weights, and effort/RPE alongside quick sessions. Detailed fields are never mandatory; attach later details to the existing session rather than double-counting the workout. |
| Feature 17: BJJ logging | Support quick logging from an editable usual-session template with warm-up, technical practice, positional work and sparring, plus optional actual effort/round details. User-provided routine details are stored privately as onboarding context, not in public examples or completed-session records. |
| Feature 18: training schedule | Keep an editable recurring weekly schedule with per-day overrides. The user's default BJJ weekdays are recorded privately. Unspecified days are not automatically rest days, and scheduled sessions never become completed logs without confirmation. |
| Feature 19: training-day nutrition | Keep both training-day calorie/carbohydrate allocation within the agreed weekly budget and meal/timing guidance around sessions. Do not automatically add estimated exercise calories to intake targets. |
| Feature 20: recovery check-ins | Keep optional sleep, fatigue, soreness, and readiness logging through text or buttons. Accept partial entries and retain missing values as unknown. Reminder preferences remain separate. |
| Feature 21: workload and recovery analysis | Keep personal workload trends and explained, optional recovery-based training suggestions when sufficient history supports them. Do not automatically change workouts or present injury/overtraining diagnoses. |
| Feature 36: supplements and phased protocols | Keep a separate reviewed supplement product, plan and actual-dose ledger. Support generic dated phases, including optional creatine loading followed by maintenance. Planned doses never count as consumed; supplement nutrients remain distinguishable from food; no AI-invented quantities, autonomous therapeutic dosing or interaction clearance. See `supplements-proposal.md`. |
| Feature 22: historical questions and summaries | Keep natural-language questions about stored history and AI-written explanations. Application code calculates factual results from validated queries; AI receives only relevant context through the shared aggregator. Preserve missing-data caveats and distinguish associations from causes. |
| Feature 23: shared AI platform | Use OpenRouter Standard as the initial shared aggregator for all AI features, including text and vision. Keep the integration replaceable. Exact models remain to be finalized; the monthly usage cap is $10. Privacy follows feature 27. |
| Feature 24: model selection strategy | Choose models by task: economical model for routine text, suitable vision model for meal/label photos, and a stronger model for complex analysis. Keep model assignments configurable and select exact models through representative task evaluation. All AI uses the shared OpenRouter integration and budget. |
| Feature 25: monthly AI budget | Set a shared $10 USD monthly model/API usage cap across all AI tasks, including retries and fallback calls. Credit-purchase fees and taxes are separate. At the cap, pause paid AI and offer an explicit increase for the current month as specified in feature 26; no automatic top-ups or increases. |
| Feature 26: behavior at AI usage cap | Pause paid AI at the usage cap and offer a Telegram action to explicitly approve a higher limit for the current month. Display the new total limit and expiry before acceptance; return to the base $10 limit next month. Local/manual logging, saved meals, and calculated reports remain available. No automatic extra spending or credit purchases. |
| Feature 27: AI privacy and fallback routing | Allow reviewed endpoints that prohibit training on submitted data, including endpoints with disclosed temporary retention. Apply the same requirements to primary and fallback routes. Send only task-relevant context and keep optional gateway content logging disabled. Unknown or incompatible policies are not eligible. |
| Feature 28: automatic reminders and summaries | Keep configurable morning weight/recovery check-ins, relevant training prompts, evening summaries, and weekly reviews, with separate category switches. Suppress completed check-in prompts, respect quiet hours, and coalesce stale reminders after downtime. The user’s times, timezone, training starts, report length, and quiet-hours choice are recorded privately; training-relative reminders follow feature 34. |
| Feature 29: data export | Keep on-demand CSV tables and structured JSON exports, with date-range and category filters, delivered as files to the authorized private Telegram chat on explicit request. Include units, source references, targets, and relevant revision history; version the JSON schema. Expire temporary export files on the server. Exports complement, rather than replace, backup/restore. |
| Feature 30: local data retention | Keep original text and photo copies on the VM for 30 days after successful processing. Keep saved structured records and correction history until explicitly deleted. Source material still needed by unresolved drafts follows the seven-day inactivity expiry in feature 35. This does not change Telegram/provider retention or historical backup expiry. |
| Feature 31: deletion controls | Keep full controls for selected entries, date ranges, categories, and all personal records. Bulk/permanent deletion previews the exact scope and affected counts and requires explicit confirmation. Distinguish reversible log removal/undo from permanent erasure. Explain external-copy and backup limitations; configuration and shared food caches are removed only when explicitly included in the displayed scope. |
| Feature 32: reminder setup timing | Configure schedules, timezone, quiet hours and report preferences explicitly and store them privately. Planned Telegram settings must allow later edits; no personal schedule is embedded in public defaults. |
| Feature 33: editable time and notification settings | Allow timezone, reminder times, training schedule, report format, category switches, and quiet hours to be changed through `/settings` and natural language. Validate values and show a saved-settings receipt. Changes apply to future schedules; preserve historical timestamps and local log dates. Recompute pending reminders and invalidate stale scheduled jobs without replaying already-delivered notifications. |
| Feature 34: training reminder timing | Keep a relevant preparation/nutrition prompt two hours before a planned session and a logging/recovery check-in fifteen minutes after its planned end. Treat planned end as approximate, never as attendance evidence. Suppress cancelled-session reminders, already-logged session prompts, and repetitive recovery questions. Keep timings editable and the separate evening summary unchanged. |
| Feature 35: unfinished draft expiry | Expire unconfirmed drafts after seven days without user activity and remove their local source copies. Preserve only minimal non-content metadata to reject stale actions. Expiry never approves a draft, changes saved records, or adds nutrition totals. Saved records follow the separate 30-day raw-source retention policy. |
| AI access approach | Use one replaceable aggregator integration with configurable task-specific models and a shared spending cap. OpenRouter is selected; the monthly usage cap is $10; actual model assignments remain open; privacy follows feature 27. |

Only the features explicitly confirmed above are finalized. Referencing templates or other possible features in the hybrid architecture does not independently approve those features.

Implementation guidance for the hybrid choice: require full-input coverage by the local parser; if it cannot account for meaningful wording, clarify or route to AI rather than silently dropping qualifiers. AI returns validated food/quantity intents, never authoritative nutrition values.

Portion approval contract:

- A specific amount supplied by the user, a package with a known amount, or an explicitly selected fixed measured portion can resolve quantity. An inferred average is still an estimate even if expressed as an exact-looking gram number.
- Present estimates with their basis (for example personal history or standard serving), food/preparation assumptions, and affected items. Offer `Enter amount`, `Approve estimate`, and `Cancel`.
- Keep uncertain meals in drafts, outside totals, until quantities are resolved or the user explicitly taps the estimate-approval button. A single tap may approve an explicitly itemized meal draft.
- Approval applies to the displayed draft revision and amounts. Changed estimates need fresh approval. Record the approving action/time, estimated quantity method, and basis with the saved revision; preserve the estimated flag in reports.
- Past approvals improve future suggestions but never provide blanket authorization for new uncertain portions. Templates containing rough quantities still need explicit estimate approval when reused; a fixed measured recipe selected by the user can follow the precise-quantity path.
- Model confidence, absence of a reply, and conversion to grams cannot turn an uncertain amount into a precise one. Approving an estimate does not make its nutritional accuracy equivalent to a measured portion.

OpenRouter was checked against its current [structured-output documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/structured-outputs) and [provider routing controls](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection). Structured-output support varies by endpoint. Proposed implementation: configurable model/provider allowlist, required-parameter support, local schema validation, bounded retries/budget, and consistent privacy restrictions on fallback routes. The selected policy permits disclosed temporary retention but prohibits training on submitted data; an aggregator adds an intermediary as well as an upstream model provider.

Correction contract: resolve the target from the replied-to receipt or an explicit selection; ask when ambiguous. Preserve revision history, support undo, and reject stale/repeated actions safely. New rough quantities introduced by an edit need explicit estimate approval before the replacement is committed. Editing a saved entry does not change the underlying reusable template unless separately requested.

Meal reuse contract: support named favorites, repeating a previous meal, and batch recipes with ingredient quantities and total cooked yield or explicitly defined servings. Version templates/recipes so updates affect future uses without rewriting historical logs. Scale nutrition by the consumed portion; keep uncertainties from ingredient quantities or yield visible and require explicit approval when reusing rough quantities.

Packaged-food contract: validate readable barcode digits and display the matched product; if unreadable or unmatched, offer typed digits, label photo, or manual entry rather than inventing a match. Label extraction uses the shared AI aggregator when needed and presents values/units/per-100g-or-serving basis for review. Preserve label/source provenance, keep omitted nutrients unknown, and apply the existing quantity-approval rule. A barcode alone never establishes how much was eaten.

## Review status

The feature review is complete. All 35 decisions above are confirmed. Exact model/endpoint assignments and operational implementation details remain engineering work; they do not reopen accepted product choices. No reminders or AI are activated. See the README for implementation scope and foundation-operations.md for verification boundaries.

Task-based model contract: local parsing remains first for recognized inputs. Route AI requests using application-defined task categories and configurable model assignments, not unrestricted model-selected routing. A stronger model can explain complex results but never replaces deterministic calculations or the estimate-approval requirement. Exact models, allowed fallback routes, and retry costs must be evaluated and constrained by the eventual shared budget and privacy choices.

BJJ template contract: preserve approximate total duration and typical session stages, but leave actual round counts and missing stage durations unknown unless supplied. Offer easy/medium/hard or numeric overall effort without forcing detailed logging. Do not treat the whole session as hard sparring, infer observed round counts from a range, or add included round minutes on top of total duration. Clarify ambiguous round notation only when needed for a detailed calculation. Personal training templates belong in private runtime data.

Quick-gym contract: preserve reported focus and duration as observations. Missing session effort may use a visible configurable typical value; use comparable personally reported efforts when sufficient history exists, without treating earlier inferred values as fresh measured evidence. Store estimate source/method and allow correction. Do not fabricate actual exercises, sets, reps, or lifted weights from averages. Session load remains approximate when effort is inferred. Interpretation for now: “averages” covers effort/load; displaying estimated exercise calories is not yet a confirmed feature, and this does not authorize automatically adding exercise calories to food targets.

Weekly report UX: `/week` offers the full report in a compact, navigable presentation. “Short weekly report,” `/week short`, or a `Short version` button returns a concise view using the same computed facts. Offer `Full report` to switch back. Keep critical data-completeness caveats and pending approval requests visible in both formats. Scheduled delivery timing and full-report preference are recorded privately; short reports remain available on request.

## Remaining engineering work

Carry the confirmed decisions into implementation; distinguish engineering defaults from user-selected product behavior.

- Execute the ordered work packages in [implementation-plan.md](implementation-plan.md).
- Evaluate the candidate assignments in [model-selection.md](model-selection.md); endpoint policy review and live evaluation remain required before AI activation.
- Configure external credentials, backup destination, and release target privately before deployment.

Authorization, deterministic calculations, reliable persistence, and verified backups remain baseline engineering requirements, not optional convenience features. Review material operational choices separately after product scope is settled.

Budget increase contract: persist the base monthly limit separately from a month-scoped override. Bind approval to the displayed new total limit and period; reject stale or duplicate actions. An override raises the permitted application usage ceiling, not the OpenRouter credit balance, and never purchases credits. Reconcile actual usage and reserve bounded request/retry costs before allowing new calls. Do not automatically replay queued requests when an override is accepted.

AI privacy contract: record the reviewed endpoint policy, retention purpose/duration where disclosed, source, and review date in deployment documentation. Show a concise policy explanation in settings. Do not equate non-training with zero retention or claim a universal retention period. Revalidate routes when policies change; if no eligible route remains, keep the draft and offer local/manual operation. Fallbacks cannot silently weaken the policy. Do not enable external search/plugins or content logging merely because a model endpoint is allowed.

Reminder contract: evaluate completed check-ins again before sending, not only when scheduling. Use configured local dates/timezone and stable logical job keys; handle DST and restart without replaying a backlog of obsolete prompts. Training-relative prompts require a known session time and must respect cancellations/overrides; weekday-only schedules do not imply a start time. A reminder never marks a meal, recovery metric, or workout as logged. All categories can be disabled independently. These are future bot features, not Codex automations to create during design.

Export contract: export only authorized user data within the requested category/date scope; include referenced food/recipe versions needed to interpret records. Exclude credentials, internal tokens, raw AI payloads, and raw photos by default. Neutralize spreadsheet formula injection in CSV text fields without altering source data; JSON preserves typed source values. Temporary files expire after delivery or bounded failure handling. Sending an export to Telegram does not make it public, but creates a separate copy outside the VM.

Local retention contract: persist successful-processing timestamps and run restart-safe cleanup of expired raw text/photos, including duplicate payload copies in inbox/AI/action storage. Preserve structured food sources, normalized quantities, user approvals, and revision metadata needed for accuracy without retaining original message bodies indefinitely inside those records. Keep only data required for unresolved drafts; drafts expire after seven days without user activity, and expiry never counts as estimate approval. The 30-day window applies to active VM data; historical backups expire on their own documented schedule.

Deletion contract: permanent erasure removes in-scope active records, revision payloads, raw inputs, derived summaries, and queued messages containing erased content; recalculate affected totals and invalidate stale callbacks. Preview dependent records before confirmation, and preserve unrelated records and shared food reference data. Keep only minimal non-content operational metadata where needed for idempotency. Existing backup snapshots expire under the backup policy; restore must include a deletion-reconciliation step before normal use so erased records are not silently reintroduced. Do not promise physical secure erasure of storage blocks or deletion from Telegram/provider systems. Permanent erasure is not undoable through the normal correction flow.

Time-settings contract: persist an IANA timezone and local wall-clock times. On timezone changes, keep chosen local clock times unless the user asks otherwise, show the resulting next notification times, and atomically invalidate/recompute unsent jobs. Preserve UTC timestamps and recorded local dates for historical entries. Timezone changes must not reset the AI usage ledger or silently rebucket historical nutrition. Reports use explicit date ranges; the scheduled weekly review covers the preceding completed local week. Keep actual preferences private. Quiet hours disabled means scheduled categories can send at their chosen times, not an invitation to send extra messages.

Draft expiry contract: persist last_user_activity_at in UTC. Explicit draft opening/editing refreshes it; background retries, reminder delivery, and provider callbacks do not. Check expiry transactionally before accepting a callback, so cleanup lag cannot allow a stale approval. Cleanup is idempotent and restart-safe, purges associated raw payload copies and stale queued content, and cannot remove source material still referenced by another valid record/draft. Preserve only non-content identifiers needed to reject stale actions. Explain expiry when an old button is used and offer a new draft; never reconstruct an approved log from expired content. Historical backup copies follow the separately documented backup policy.
